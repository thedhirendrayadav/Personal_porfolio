01 / SECURITY

IN DEVELOPMENT

Threat-model workbench

RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS
A compact asset-and-boundary worksheet can expose abuse paths before implementation choices become expensive.
METHOD
Map actors, assets, entry points, trust crossings, misuse cases, and proposed controls.
EVIDENCE TARGET
A reviewable model that connects each risk to an owner, control, and verification step.
CONSTRAINT
The model supports decisions; it does not prove a system is secure.

02 / AUTOMATION

IN DEVELOPMENT

Detection workflow

RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS
Alerts become more actionable when collection, context, prioritization, and human review are visible in one flow.
METHOD
Use synthetic events to exercise normalization, rule evaluation, evidence capture, and escalation states.
EVIDENCE TARGET
Repeatable test cases showing why an event was promoted, suppressed, or sent for review.
CONSTRAINT
No claim of live monitoring coverage is made without a deployed data source.

03 / APPLIED AI

IN DEVELOPMENT

Retrieval-grounded assistant

RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS
Answers are more useful when every response is bounded by approved sources and exposes its supporting context.
METHOD
Compare retrieval, source filtering, refusal behavior, and citation coverage on a controlled document set.
EVIDENCE TARGET
Evaluation cases for grounded answers, missing context, conflicting sources, and safe refusal.
CONSTRAINT
Generated output remains advisory and requires verification for consequential decisions.

04 / OPERATIONS

IN DEVELOPMENT

Deployment observability

RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS
A small release is safer when health, configuration, migrations, and rollback evidence are treated as one deployment record.
METHOD
Exercise readiness checks, structured logs, failure states, and recovery notes against a controlled service.
EVIDENCE TARGET
A concise release ledger showing what changed, what was checked, and how recovery would proceed.
CONSTRAINT
Local checks do not establish production reliability without live operational evidence.
